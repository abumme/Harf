One word a day, in a script that is actually yours. The whole product turns on a small linguistic stubbornness — that a tile should be one letter as a speaker counts letters, not one Unicode character. What follows is a paper prototype of the six screens that carry that idea, in the order a player meets them.

01 The board

A tile is a grapheme, not a character

shahar (“city”) is five tiles, sh · a · h · a · r, even though it is six characters. The custom keyboard gives digraphs their own keys (highlighted), so the player never types s then h and hopes. Input ambiguity simply never exists.

The sharpest bet is Uzbek’s two scripts. The daily word is one lexeme, rendered in whichever script you chose.

Try it → Toggle Lotin / Кирилл on the board. Same word, same day, two renderings: shahar ↔ шаҳар. League scoring is by guess count, so the two boards still compete fairly.

02 The share

The card is the product

The emoji grid is copy-paste-proof and language-agnostic: five tiles read the same whether the word was six Latin characters or five Cyrillic ones. That uniformity is the whole point — a Tashkent league and a Moscow league post identical-looking grids.

Every card carries a deep link. Installed, it opens straight to today’s puzzle; not installed, it lands on the store, and the referrer is captured, which is what makes “installs per share” a real number.

Loop 2, the K-factor. Over-invest here. The card as seen inside a Telegram group is the true first surface of the product.
03 The habit

A streak worth protecting

Local-first, mirrored to the server, because gifting only works if the server knows the streak is at risk. The number on this screen is the emotional asset every other mechanic leans on.

Guess distribution, win rate, best streak: the quiet retention furniture that gives the daily open a reason beyond novelty.

Event-sourced. Streaks are replayed from a result log, not a mutable counter, so an offline solve and a gifted save can arrive in any order and still reconcile.

04 The stakes

Gift a friend’s streak back

The weekly league is turn-based: no realtime, just a table that updates as results come in. Its job is to make a friend’s broken streak visible to you.

Seeing it, you can spend $0.49 to save it. The purchase runs through RevenueCat → a webhook grants the repair → a push tells them “Aziz saved your streak.” Both of you re-engage. This is the headline monetization story.

The boundary feature. The only MVP mechanic needing the full server-side entitlement chain, so it gets a protected engineering week for exactly that reason.
05 First run

Three screens, then play

Language, then script (Uzbek only), then a notification ask deferred until after the first solve. Nothing between a curious tap and the board that keeps it short enough to survive the store’s crowded first impression.

Positioning starts here. Seeing your own language on the first screen is the entire “finally, a word game in our language” pitch, delivered before a single word is typed.

06 Money, gently

No wall, three streams

The core game is free forever. Revenue comes from a one-time Founder unlock, the $0.49 streak gifts, and a few $0.99 board themes visible on the league table: status, not obstruction.

Deferred with intent. Gems, season passes and seasonal packs are named in the roadmap, not shipped at launch. A story told honestly beats a paywall told loudly.
